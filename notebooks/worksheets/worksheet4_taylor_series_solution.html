Worksheet 4: Taylor series solution#

This is a pencil and paper problem about expanding the equation of state in a Taylor series

Tayor series examples#

One dimension#

From the equation sheet:

\[\begin{split} f(x) &=& f(x_0) + f^\prime(x_0)(x - x_0) \nonumber\\ &+& \frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x_0)}{2}(x-x_0)^2 + \ldots \end{split}\]

Example:#

Show that to first order, if x is close to 0:

\[ \frac{1}{1-x} \approx 1 + x \]

Answer#

\[\begin{split} f(x_0) = \frac{1}{(1 - 0)} = 1 \\ f^\prime(x_0) = -(1 - x_0)^{-2} \times (-1) = 1 \\ f^{\prime \prime}(x_0) = (-2) \times (1 - x_0)^{-3} \times (-1) = -2 \\ f(x) = 1 + x - \frac{2}{2} x^2 + \ldots = 1 + x - x^2 + \ldots \end{split}\]

Two dimensions: Taylor’s series expansion of the equation of state#

Consider this equation from the Wikipedia entry on Taylor series

(2)#\[\begin{split} \begin{align} f(x,y) & \approx f(a,b) +(x-a)\, f_x(a,b) +(y-b)\, f_y(a,b)\nonumber \\ & + \frac{1}{2!}\left[ (x-a)^2\,f_{xx}(a,b) + 2(x-a)(y-b)\,f_{xy}(a,b) +(y-b)^2\, f_{yy}(a,b) \right] \end{align} \end{split}\]

where \(f_{xy} = \frac{ \partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y }\), etc. If you expand \(f=p=\rho R_d T\) about the point \(p_0(z) = \rho_0(z) R_d T_0(z)\) where \(p_0,\ \rho_0,\ T_0\) are the pressure, density and temperature at height \(z\) for a hydrostatic atmosphere. Using (2) with \(a=\rho_0\) and \(b=T_0\) and \(f(a,b) = p_0\) you should be able to show that to second order:

(3)#\[ \frac{\Delta p}{p_0} = \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} + \frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho_0} + \frac{\Delta T \Delta \rho}{T_0 \rho_0} \]

Where \(\Delta p = p - p_0\), etc. Note that \(\Delta p\), \(\Delta T\), and \(\Delta \rho\) are all functions of (t,x,y,z).

If the atmosphere is close to hydrostatic balance, then we can expect the \(\Delta\) differences to be small if \(p_0\) is the hydrostatic pressure, and we can drop the \(\frac{ \Delta T \Delta \rho}{T_0 \rho_0}\) term and write

(4)#\[ \frac{\Delta p}{p_0} = \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} + \frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho_0} \]

We will show later that away from active convection we also can expect \(\frac{\Delta p }{p_0}\) to be small.

Solution: show that (4) is true given (2):#

Start with finding the partial derivatives need for (2)

(5)#\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} & \frac{\partial p}{\partial T}=R_d \rho \\ & \frac{\partial p}{\partial \rho}=R_d T \\ & \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial T^2}=0 \\ & \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial \rho^2}=0 \\ & \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial \rho \,\partial T}=R_d \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

So now taking \(x = \rho\), \(y = T\) in (2), and evaluating the derivatives at the hydrostatic base state \(a=\rho_0\), \(b=T_0\), (2) becomes:

(6)#\[ \begin{align} p - p_0 & \approx (\rho- \rho_0)\, R_d T_0 + (T-T_0)\, R_d \rho_0 + (\rho-\rho_0)(T-T_0)R_d \end{align} \]

Finally, divide both sides by \(p_0 = R_d \rho_0 T_0\) and use the \(\Delta\) notation:

\[ \frac{\Delta p}{p_0} \approx \frac{\Delta \rho R_d T_0}{ R_d \rho_0 T_0} + \frac{\Delta T\,R_d \rho_0}{R_d \rho_0 T_0} + \frac{\Delta \rho\, \Delta T\,R_d}{R_d \rho_0 T_0} \]

Cancelling terms in the numerator and denominator, we get (4)

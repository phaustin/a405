Worksheet 4: Taylor series solution

This is a pencil and paper problem about expanding the equation of state in a Taylor series

Tayor series examples¶

One dimension¶

From the equation sheet:

f(x)=f(x0)+f′(x0)(x−x0)+f′′(x0)2(x−x0)2+…\begin{aligned} f(x) &= f(x_0) + f^\prime(x_0)(x - x_0)\\ &+ \frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x_0)}{2}(x-x_0)^2 + \ldots \end{aligned}f(x)​=f(x0​)+f′(x0​)(x−x0​)+2f′′(x0​)​(x−x0​)2+…​
(1)

Example:¶

Show that to first order, if x is close to 0:

11−x≈1+x\frac{1}{1-x} \approx 1 + x1−x1​≈1+x
(2)

Answer¶

f(x0)=1(1−0)=1f′(x0)=−(1−x0)−2×(−1)=1f′′(x0)=(−2)×(1−x0)−3×(−1)=−2f(x)=1+x−22x2+…=1+x−x2+…f(x_0) = \frac{1}{(1 - 0)} = 1 \\ f^\prime(x_0) = -(1 - x_0)^{-2} \times (-1) = 1 \\ f^{\prime \prime}(x_0) = (-2) \times (1 - x_0)^{-3} \times (-1) = -2 \\ f(x) = 1 + x - \frac{2}{2} x^2 + \ldots = 1 + x - x^2 + \ldotsf(x0​)=(1−0)1​=1f′(x0​)=−(1−x0​)−2×(−1)=1f′′(x0​)=(−2)×(1−x0​)−3×(−1)=−2f(x)=1+x−22​x2+…=1+x−x2+…
(3)

Two dimensions: Taylor’s series expansion of the equation of state¶

Consider this equation from the Wikipedia entry on Taylor series

f(x,y)≈f(a,b)+(x−a) fx(a,b)+(y−b) fy(a,b)+12![(x−a)2 fxx(a,b)+2(x−a)(y−b) fxy(a,b)+(y−b)2 fyy(a,b)]\begin{align} f(x,y) & \approx f(a,b) +(x-a)\, f_x(a,b) +(y-b)\, f_y(a,b)\nonumber \\ & + \frac{1}{2!}\left[ (x-a)^2\,f_{xx}(a,b) + 2(x-a)(y-b)\,f_{xy}(a,b) +(y-b)^2\, f_{yy}(a,b) \right] \end{align}f(x,y)​≈f(a,b)+(x−a)fx​(a,b)+(y−b)fy​(a,b)+2!1​[(x−a)2fxx​(a,b)+2(x−a)(y−b)fxy​(a,b)+(y−b)2fyy​(a,b)]​​
(4)

where fxy=∂2f∂x∂yf_{xy} = \frac{ \partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y }fxy​=∂x∂y∂2f​, etc. If you expand f=p=ρRdTf=p=\rho R_d Tf=p=ρRd​T about the point p0(z)=ρ0(z)RdT0(z)p_0(z) = \rho_0(z) R_d T_0(z)p0​(z)=ρ0​(z)Rd​T0​(z) where p0, ρ0, T0p_0,\ \rho_0,\ T_0p0​, ρ0​, T0​ are the pressure, density and temperature at height zzz for a hydrostatic atmosphere. Using (4) with a=ρ0a=\rho_0a=ρ0​ and b=T0b=T_0b=T0​ and f(a,b)=p0f(a,b) = p_0f(a,b)=p0​ you should be able to show that to second order:

Δpp0=ΔTT0+Δρρ0+ΔTΔρT0ρ0\frac{\Delta p}{p_0} = \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} + \frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho_0} + \frac{\Delta T \Delta \rho}{T_0 \rho_0}p0​Δp​=T0​ΔT​+ρ0​Δρ​+T0​ρ0​ΔTΔρ​
(5)

Where Δp=p−p0\Delta p = p - p_0Δp=p−p0​, etc. Note that Δp\Delta pΔp, ΔT\Delta TΔT, and Δρ\Delta \rhoΔρ are all functions of (t,x,y,z).

If the atmosphere is close to hydrostatic balance, then we can expect the Δ\DeltaΔ differences to be small if p0p_0p0​ is the hydrostatic pressure, and we can drop the ΔTΔρT0ρ0\frac{ \Delta T \Delta \rho}{T_0 \rho_0}T0​ρ0​ΔTΔρ​ term and write

Δpp0=ΔTT0+Δρρ0\frac{\Delta p}{p_0} = \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} + \frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho_0}p0​Δp​=T0​ΔT​+ρ0​Δρ​
(6)

We will show later that away from active convection we also can expect Δpp0\frac{\Delta p }{p_0}p0​Δp​ to be small.

Solution: show that (6) is true given (4):¶

Start with finding the partial derivatives need for (4)

∂p∂T=Rdρ∂p∂ρ=RdT∂2p∂T2=0∂2p∂ρ2=0∂2p∂ρ ∂T=Rd\begin{aligned} & \frac{\partial p}{\partial T}=R_d \rho \\ & \frac{\partial p}{\partial \rho}=R_d T \\ & \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial T^2}=0 \\ & \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial \rho^2}=0 \\ & \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial \rho \,\partial T}=R_d \end{aligned}​∂T∂p​=Rd​ρ∂ρ∂p​=Rd​T∂T2∂2p​=0∂ρ2∂2p​=0∂ρ∂T∂2p​=Rd​​
(7)

So now taking x=ρx = \rhox=ρ, y=Ty = Ty=T in (4), and evaluating the derivatives at the hydrostatic base state a=ρ0a=\rho_0a=ρ0​, b=T0b=T_0b=T0​, (4) becomes:

p−p0≈(ρ−ρ0) RdT0+(T−T0) Rdρ0+(ρ−ρ0)(T−T0)Rd\begin{align} p - p_0 & \approx (\rho- \rho_0)\, R_d T_0 + (T-T_0)\, R_d \rho_0 + (\rho-\rho_0)(T-T_0)R_d \end{align}p−p0​​≈(ρ−ρ0​)Rd​T0​+(T−T0​)Rd​ρ0​+(ρ−ρ0​)(T−T0​)Rd​​​
(8)

Finally, divide both sides by p0=Rdρ0T0p_0 = R_d \rho_0 T_0p0​=Rd​ρ0​T0​ and use the Δ\DeltaΔ notation:

Δpp0≈ΔρRdT0Rdρ0T0+ΔT Rdρ0Rdρ0T0+Δρ ΔT RdRdρ0T0\frac{\Delta p}{p_0} \approx \frac{\Delta \rho R_d T_0}{ R_d \rho_0 T_0} + \frac{\Delta T\,R_d \rho_0}{R_d \rho_0 T_0} + \frac{\Delta \rho\, \Delta T\,R_d}{R_d \rho_0 T_0}p0​Δp​≈Rd​ρ0​T0​ΔρRd​T0​​+Rd​ρ0​T0​ΔTRd​ρ0​​+Rd​ρ0​T0​ΔρΔTRd​​
(9)

Cancelling terms in the numerator and denominator, we get (6)

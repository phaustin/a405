Week1: Hydrostatic balance explainer

This note goes into more detail on the idea of hydrostatic balance, which is introduced in Stull Chapter 1, p. 16 eq. 1.25b or Thompkins p. 10 eq. 1.29.

One major simplification for atmospheric thermodyamics is the fact that on timescales lareger than 30 minute or so the environment is in hydrostatic balance, which is the statement that the vertical pressure differential across a layer provides exactly the amount of force necessary to balance gravity, so the atmosphere is not rising or sinking:

In symbols, the balance shown by Figure 1 implies that:

dp=−ρgdzdp = - \rho g dzdp=−ρgdz
(1)

Question: is this the same pressure p as the local pressure given by the equation of state: p=ρRdTp=\rho R_d Tp=ρRd​T?

Scale Heights¶

On page 10 equation 1.30 Thompkins integrates the hydrostatic equation to get the pressure as a function of height, assuming that temperature is constant with height. That’s not a great assumption. Here’s how to do a better job by defining a height-averaged temperature that gives the exact answer. We want to find the pressure scale height HpH_pHp​ such that:

p=p0exp⁡(−zHp)p = p_0 \exp \left ( \frac{-z}{H_p} \right )p=p0​exp(Hp​−z​)
(2)

In words -- HpH_pHp​ gives the height at which gravity wins out over the kinetic energy of the air molecules -- warmer atmospheres take longer to decrease their pressure by a factor of exp⁡(−Hp/Hp)=exp⁡(−1)\exp(-H_p/H_p) = \exp(-1)exp(−Hp​/Hp​)=exp(−1) (the e-folding height) because the air molecules have more energy to travel higher.

  1. Do pressure first: Rewrite (1) using the ideal gas law:

    dp=−ρgdz=−pRdTgdz=pHpdp = - \rho g dz = - \frac{p}{R_d T} g dz = \frac{p}{H_p}dp=−ρgdz=−Rd​Tp​gdz=Hp​p​
    (3)

    where Hp=RdT/gH_p=R_d T/gHp​=Rd​T/g is the pressure scale height.

    dln⁡p=−dzHpd\ln p = - \frac{dz }{H_p}dlnp=−Hp​dz​
    (4)
    ∫p0p ⁣ dln⁡p=−∫0z ⁣1Hpdz′\int_{p_0}^{p}\!\,d \ln p = - \int_{0 }^{z}\!\frac{1}{H_p} dz^\prime∫p0​p​dlnp=−∫0z​Hp​1​dz′
    (5)

    Since temperature (and gravity) are changing with height, we need to know profiles of g(z) and T(z) to go any further. If we don’t have those profiles, then a useful approximation is to define a vertically averaged HpH_pHp​, since the average involves a large mass of air (about 10 metric tons/cubic meter), it will vary more slowly than the temperature at any one level on any given day, and we can still get a useful profile of pressure vs. height without having a specific sounding:

    1Hp‾=(1Hp)‾=∫0z ⁣1Hpdz′z−0\frac{ 1}{\overline{H_p}} = \overline{ \left ( \frac{1 }{H_p} \right )} = \frac{\int_{0 }^{z}\!\frac{1}{H_p} dz^\prime }{z-0}Hp​​1​=(Hp​1​)​=z−0∫0z​Hp​1​dz′​
    (6)

    Now we can replace the z integral by the average:

    ∫p0p ⁣ dln⁡p=−∫0z ⁣1Hpdz′=−zHp‾\int_{p_0}^{p}\!\,d \ln p = - \int_{0 }^{z}\!\frac{1}{H_p} dz^\prime = -\frac{z}{\overline{H_p}}∫p0​p​dlnp=−∫0z​Hp​1​dz′=−Hp​​z​
    (7)

    and integrating the left-hand side:

    ln⁡p/p0=−zHp‾\ln p/p_0 = - \frac{z }{\overline{H_p}}lnp/p0​=−Hp​​z​
    (8)

    Taking exp of both sides gives the pressure with height:

    p=p0exp⁡(−zH‾p)p = p_0 \exp \left ( - \frac{z }{\overline H_p} \right )p=p0​exp(−Hp​z​)
    (9)

    Values for Hp‾\overline{H_p}Hp​​ are relatively constant for a particular climate regime, like midlatitude winter.

  2. Now repeat this for density ρ\rhoρ. We need to use the chain rule for the equation of state:

    dpdz=ddz(ρRdT)=Rd(dρdzT+ρdTdz)=−ρg\frac{dp }{dz} = \frac{d }{dz} (\rho R_d T) = R_d \left ( \frac{d\rho }{dz} T + \rho \frac{ dT}{dz} \right ) = - \rho gdzdp​=dzd​(ρRd​T)=Rd​(dzdρ​T+ρdzdT​)=−ρg
    (10)
    dρdz=−ρT(gRd+dTdz)=−ρ(1H+1TdTdz)=−ρHρ\frac{d\rho }{dz} = -\frac{\rho }{T} \left ( \frac{g }{R_d} + \frac{ dT}{dz} \right ) = - \rho \left ( \frac{1 }{H} + \frac{1 }{T} \frac{dT }{dz} \right ) = - \frac{\rho}{H_\rho}dzdρ​=−Tρ​(Rd​g​+dzdT​)=−ρ(H1​+T1​dzdT​)=−Hρ​ρ​
    (11)

    Bottom line -- with this definition of the density scale height we’ve got an equation that looks like (3):

    dρρ=−(1H+1TdTdz)dz=−dzHρ\frac{d\rho }{\rho} = - \left ( \frac{1 }{H} + \frac{1 }{T} \frac{dT }{dz} \right ) dz = - \frac{dz }{H_\rho}ρdρ​=−(H1​+T1​dzdT​)dz=−Hρ​dz​
    (12)

    Pull the same trick for the vertical average:

    1Hρ‾=(1Hρ)‾=∫0z ⁣1Hρdz′z−0\frac{ 1}{\overline{H_\rho}} = \overline{ \left ( \frac{1 }{H_\rho} \right )} = \frac{\int_{0 }^{z}\!\frac{1}{H_\rho} dz^\prime }{z-0}Hρ​​1​=(Hρ​1​)​=z−0∫0z​Hρ​1​dz′​
    (13)

    We use this vertical average in exactly the same way as before to get the density profile:

    ln⁡ρ/ρ0=−zHρ‾ρ=ρ0exp⁡(−zH‾ρ)\begin{aligned} \ln \rho/\rho_0 =& - \frac{z }{\overline{H_\rho }} \\ \rho =& \rho_0 \exp \left ( - \frac{z }{\overline H_\rho} \right )\end{aligned}lnρ/ρ0​=ρ=​−Hρ​​z​ρ0​exp(−Hρ​z​)​
    (14)

    p=poexp⁡(−z/Hp)p = p_o \exp ( -z/H_p)p=po​exp(−z/Hp​)
